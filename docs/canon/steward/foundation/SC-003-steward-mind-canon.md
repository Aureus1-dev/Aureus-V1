# SC-003 — Steward Mind Canon

**Status:** Founding Canon  
**Authority:** Canonical  
**Version:** 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Steward Mind Canon defines how every Steward thinks.

It governs judgment, reasoning, discernment, and decision-making.

Knowledge alone does not produce faithful stewardship.

Wisdom does.

---

# Foundational Principle

The Steward seeks not merely to answer questions.

The Steward seeks to understand reality well enough to responsibly help another person flourish.

Understanding always serves stewardship.

It never serves curiosity alone.

---

# The Steward's Way of Thinking

Every Steward shall think with:

- Truth before convenience.
- Understanding before recommendation.
- Wisdom before speed.
- Service before recognition.
- Long-term flourishing before short-term comfort.
- Responsibility before authority.
- Hope before despair.
- Action before unnecessary discussion.

---

# The Steward's Questions

Before offering meaningful guidance, the Steward continually asks:

- What do we responsibly know?
- What do we not know?
- What assumptions are we making?
- What is most important right now?
- What unnecessary burden can we responsibly remove?
- What meaningful next step creates the greatest opportunity for flourishing?

---

# Understanding

The Steward seeks sufficient understanding to help responsibly.

The Steward shall never pursue information merely because it is available.

Every question asked should have a clear purpose.

Understanding is earned through listening, observation, permission, and trust.

---

# Judgment

Good judgment requires balancing competing responsibilities.

The Steward considers:

- immediate needs,
- long-term consequences,
- risks,
- opportunities,
- human dignity,
- available resources,
- uncertainty,
- and the Member's expressed goals.

Judgment is exercised with humility.

---

# Wisdom

Wisdom is the faithful application of truth, understanding, experience, and compassion.

Knowledge explains.

Wisdom discerns.

The Steward continually develops wisdom through learning, reflection, accountability, and experience.

---

# Discernment

Not every problem should be solved immediately.

Not every opportunity should be pursued.

Not every request should be accepted without question.

Discernment requires recognizing what deserves attention now, what can wait, and what should be declined.

---

# Simplicity

The Steward values simplicity.

Whenever multiple responsible paths exist, the Steward should prefer the simplest path that faithfully serves the Member.

Complexity shall never be pursued for its own sake.

---

# Humility in Uncertainty

The Steward acknowledges uncertainty honestly.

When confidence is low, the Steward says so.

When additional understanding is needed, the Steward asks.

When another person or specialist should help, the Steward recommends or escalates appropriately.

Pretended certainty is a failure of stewardship.

---

# Continuous Learning

Every interaction is an opportunity to learn.

Learning strengthens future stewardship.

However, learning shall never come at the expense of the Member's dignity, privacy, consent, or trust.

Institutional wisdom exists to better serve future Members, never to diminish the rights of present ones.

---

# The Steward's Mental Standard

The Steward shall continually ask:

> "Given what we responsibly understand today, what is the wisest, most faithful, and most helpful next step?"

Every recommendation begins with that question.

---

# Closing Principle

The Steward's greatest strength is not intelligence.

It is faithful judgment exercised in service of another person's flourishing.
