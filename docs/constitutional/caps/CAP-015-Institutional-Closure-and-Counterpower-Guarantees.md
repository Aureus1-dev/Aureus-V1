## 1. Purpose

This CAP proposes a new constitutional supplement, **OAS-012 — Institutional Closure and Counterpower Guarantees**, to close the bounded institutional defects identified through three adversarial constitutional review passes without reopening Aureus's settled constitutional architecture.

Its purpose is to make constitutional restraints operational where Draft III identified incomplete process, emergency, counterpower, funding, safeguarding, evidence-integrity, anti-evasion, and maintenance machinery.

This CAP is intentionally institution-facing. It does **not** enact, accelerate, infer, or ratify any permanent termination, surrender, transfer, expiry, or redefinition of authority held personally by the Founder. Those matters remain reserved for separate Founder reading and later decision.

---

## 2. Current constitutional text affected

The current Constitution already establishes dignity, agency, rights, separation of powers, constitutional review, amendment discipline, emergency continuity, constitutional recordkeeping, and founding-period Founder authority. This CAP does not repeal those foundations.

It supplies additional controlling constitutional rules where the existing corpus is incomplete or materially less protective, including:

- heightened constitutional process;
- member initiative;
- Mission Protector powers;
- Tribunal removal and independence;
- continuous Assembly anti-capture requirements;
- safeguarding;
- Class A / Class B emergency controls and automatic reversion;
- cumulative emergency limits;
- anti-delay and anti-evasion rules;
- counterpower funding;
- Steward-confidentiality access limits;
- prohibition on engagement incentives in human as well as software systems;
- member-created value disputes;
- abundance measurement;
- independent-critic funding;
- enforcement following authority rather than org chart;
- service-floor solvency procedure;
- sortition participation architecture;
- dependency and exit review;
- acquisition, transfer, and jurisdictional continuity;
- omissions and non-decisions;
- constitutional evidence integrity;
- protected dissent;
- constitutional integrity and cross-reference registers;
- plain-language constitutional companion; and
- plural adversarial review.

Where OAS-012 is later canonized and an older non-entrenched provision cannot be reconciled with it, the later and more specific protection controls to the extent necessary to resolve the conflict, subject always to FOUNDATION-001 through FOUNDATION-003 and any entrenched provision of OAS-001.

## 3. Proposed constitutional text

# OAS-012 — Institutional Closure and Counterpower Guarantees

## Article 1 — Relationship to Existing Constitutional Law

1. This document supplements the existing Constitution. It shall be read to preserve, strengthen, and operationalize existing rights and restraints rather than silently narrow them.
2. FOUNDATION-001 through FOUNDATION-003 remain supreme according to the governing hierarchy.
3. Entrenched provisions remain controlling.
4. A conflict between this document and an older non-entrenched constitutional provision shall be resolved by preserving the stronger protection against concentration, coercion, unreviewable authority, dependency, hidden change, or denial of remedy, unless the text unmistakably requires another result.
5. No subordinate policy, contract, product requirement, architecture, model, prompt, work order, implementation, or precedent may override this document.

## Article 2 — Functional Translation Record

For each material constitutional mechanism Aureus shall maintain, in a discoverable constitutional artifact:

1. Purpose;
2. Failure Mode;
3. Assumptions; and
4. Present Mechanism.

Assumptions shall identify material technical, economic, cultural, legal, political, demographic, institutional, and behavioral premises. A future implementation may replace the Present Mechanism only where the applicable constitutional process establishes that the replacement preserves or strengthens the Purpose against the named Failure Mode under the then-current Assumptions.

## Article 3 — Interpretation of Qualified Duties

A constitutional qualifier such as reasonable, responsible, proportionate, necessary, practicable, material, appropriate, or similar language grants only the discretion necessary to apply the duty honestly to circumstances. It does not convert a binding duty into an aspiration.

An unexplained departure from a binding constitutional duty has no presumption of validity.

The actor relying on a qualifier bears the burden of identifying the fact, risk, conflict, impossibility, or competing constitutional duty that justifies the departure and of showing that the departure was no broader than necessary.

## Article 4 — Heightened Constitutional Process

### 4.1 Tier I — Protected Constitutional Action

Tier I applies to a material change to a protected constitutional mechanism that does not amend an entrenched right, reduce the Service Floor, abolish a counterpower, or enlarge Aureus's constitutional authority over persons.

It requires:

1. publication of the complete proposal and reasons at least thirty days before decision;
2. identification of the constitutional authority relied upon;
3. a statement of expected benefits, risks, affected rights, and alternatives;
4. an independent constitutional-impact assessment;
5. approval by two-thirds of the voting Member Assembly;
6. approval by a majority of the Constitutional Tribunal; and
7. publication of votes, dissents, evidence, and implementation conditions.

### 4.2 Tier II — Entrenched Constitutional Action

Tier II applies to:

- reduction of the constitutional Service Floor;
- amendment of entrenched rights;
- abolition or material weakening of exit;
- abolition or material weakening of a constitutional counterpower;
- material enlargement of centralized constitutional authority;
- change of control;
- transfer of mission-critical constitutional assets;
- alteration of the amendment system itself; or
- another matter expressly designated for the highest constitutional process.

It requires:

1. sixty days' public notice;
2. publication in legal and plain-language form;
3. an independent constitutional-impact assessment;
4. a cost and solvency assessment where relevant;
5. approval by three-fourths of the Member Assembly;
6. approval by at least five members of a seven-member Constitutional Tribunal;
7. concurrence by two-thirds of Mission Protectors acting only within their enumerated authority;
8. opportunity for formal public dissent;
9. disclosure of conflicts of interest; and
10. publication of the complete decision record.

No emergency authority may substitute for Tier II except where the Constitution expressly permits a temporary emergency action.

A lower process may not be substituted because the required process is inconvenient, expensive, slow, unpopular, commercially costly, or likely to reject the proposal.

## Article 5 — Significant Amendments and Member Initiative

1. Unless a more protective rule applies, a significant constitutional amendment requires two-thirds approval of the Member Assembly and two-thirds approval of the Constitutional Tribunal.
2. An amendment affecting an entrenched right, the Service Floor, constitutional exit, a constitutional counterpower, the amendment process, or institution-wide concentration of authority requires Tier II.
3. No body may unilaterally amend the scope of its own constitutional authority.
4. Members may initiate a constitutional proposal upon support from one percent of active members, subject to an absolute maximum of five hundred verified supporters.
5. The threshold authorizes consideration, not enactment.
6. A qualifying initiative shall receive acknowledgment, publication, constitutional classification, a response from the responsible body, and a recorded disposition.
7. Fees, technical barriers, unreasonable verification, or delay may not be used to raise the effective initiative threshold.

## Article 6 — Mission Protectors

Mission Protectors possess only authority expressly granted by the Constitution.

Their protective authority includes review of and, where the Constitution permits, withholding concurrence from:

1. change of control of Aureus or a mission-critical constitutional entity;
2. sale, transfer, encumbrance, abandonment, or material disposition of mission-critical constitutional assets above the applicable threshold;
3. alteration of the Mission or constitutionally entrenched mission protections;
4. dissolution or material defunding of the Tribunal, Member Assembly, Member Advocate, independent audit, outcomes function, external critic, red-team function, or another protected counterpower;
5. transfer, destruction, consolidation, or disabling of constitutional credentials, constitutional custody mechanisms, or mission-lock assets;
6. transactions principally designed to evade constitutional custody, constitutional independence, or mission restrictions; and
7. dissolution where the Constitution assigns them a protective role.

Mission Protectors possess no general executive authority. They may not operate Aureus, direct ordinary services, appoint themselves to management, substitute their policy preferences for lawful institutional decisions, or enlarge their jurisdiction by interpretation.

Where an unforeseen existential threat falls outside the enumeration, Mission Protectors may petition the Tribunal. The Tribunal may authorize only the minimum temporary protective act necessary where the threat is genuinely existential, no enumerated power is adequate, ordinary amendment cannot occur in time, and reasons are published as soon as safely possible. Temporary authorization outside the enumeration does not permanently enlarge Mission Protector authority.

## Article 7 — Tribunal Independence and Removal

1. The Constitutional Tribunal shall continuously have at least seven voting members, at least three genuinely independent appointment sources, no appointment source controlling more than one-third of seats, staggered substantial non-renewable terms, protected funding, independent access to counsel and technical expertise, authority to obtain constitutionally required evidence, and secure means of communicating, investigating, publishing, and preserving records.
2. A Tribunal member may be removed only for defined cause, including serious corruption, incapacity materially preventing service, substantial undisclosed conflict, knowing constitutional misconduct, serious breach of fiduciary or confidentiality duty, sustained abandonment of office, or comparable conduct incompatible with independent adjudication.
3. Disagreement with a lawful ruling, dissent, unpopular interpretation, criticism of Aureus, refusal to accommodate leadership preference, or a decision adverse to institutional interests is not cause.
4. Removal requires written charges, reasonable opportunity to answer, independent factual investigation, publication consistent with legitimate privacy and safety, two-thirds approval of the Member Assembly, and two-thirds concurrence of Mission Protectors.
5. A member whose removal is under consideration may not adjudicate the matter.
6. Retaliatory removal is void.

## Article 8 — Member Assembly Anti-Capture and Participation

1. A majority of voting Assembly seats shall at all times derive from sortition or another method independently certified as equal-or-stronger against capture.
2. The requirement is continuous and does not expire after founding.
3. Replacement requires certified equal-or-stronger protection.
4. Sortition administration shall provide replacement sampling, conflict screening, reasonable compensation where appropriate, accessibility accommodation, caregiving and scheduling support where reasonably necessary, language access, protection from retaliation, privacy or anonymity where constitutionally appropriate, safeguards against coercive public exposure, and reporting of invitation, acceptance, declination, and attrition rates.
5. Where participation patterns create substantial self-selection bias, the Assembly shall address the distortion rather than describing the resulting body as representative without qualification.

## Article 9 — Safeguarding Floor

Safeguarding law shall be operative before Aureus serves any child, vulnerable person, or person whose circumstances materially impair the ability to protect their own interests.

At minimum:

1. children require lawful parent or guardian authorization where required and affirmative age-appropriate assent where reasonably possible;
2. silence or dependence is not consent;
3. safeguarding concerns reach an accountable human capable of acting;
4. roles involving direct access to protected persons receive lawful screening appropriate to the role;
5. abuse, exploitation, grooming, coercion, trafficking, neglect, and imminent serious harm have defined escalation routes;
6. confidentiality exceptions are narrow, lawful, documented, and limited to protection needs;
7. commercial interest never determines whether a safeguarding concern is escalated;
8. retaliation for safeguarding reporting is prohibited;
9. records preserve enough evidence for investigation while respecting legitimate privacy;
10. Aureus distinguishes support from emergency response and never falsely represents itself as an emergency service;
11. urgent safety direction is not buried behind ordinary workflow; and
12. serious safeguarding failure receives independent review proportionate to consequence.

Nothing in this Article creates generalized surveillance authority.

## Article 10 — Protected Mechanisms in Emergencies

Protected mechanisms are divided for emergency purposes into Class A and Class B.

### Class A — Integrity of Constitutional Restraint

The following shall not ordinarily be relaxed by operational emergency authority:

- reviewer independence;
- audit-log integrity;
- evidence provenance and tamper resistance;
- independent key custody;
- revocation authority;
- rollback authority;
- constitutional access to evidence;
- the standards by which a governed system is judged safe or authorized;
- constitutional appeal and remedy machinery; and
- independence of a constitutional counterpower.

Where an emergency cannot safely be managed without weakening Class A, the presumptive response is degradation, isolation, suspension, rollback, or stopping of the affected capability.

A Class A control may be temporarily relaxed only where stopping the capability would create a demonstrably greater immediate threat, and only by Tribunal authorization on the record. Authorization expires within twenty-four hours and may be renewed once for no more than twenty-four additional hours upon fresh findings. No operations officer may self-authorize a Class A relaxation.

### Class B — Operational Protection

Permission boundaries, operational thresholds, parameters, rate limits, deployment conditions, and comparable protections may use the emergency-change route where delay presents greater immediate risk.

Each Class B emergency change is logged, justified, identifies rollback conditions, receives independent review within seven days, and automatically reverts at the end of the review period unless affirmatively confirmed through the ordinary authorized process.

Emergency authority is a temporary exception, not an accelerated permanent-amendment path.

## Article 11 — Cumulative Emergency Limits

1. Initial emergency authority may operate for no more than thirty days.
2. One renewal of up to thirty additional days may occur after the constitutionally required independent review.
3. Days sixty-one through ninety require express concurrence of both the Constitutional Tribunal and Member Assembly upon a finding that ordinary authority remains inadequate and continuation is strictly necessary.
4. No emergency condition may authorize more than ninety cumulative days of emergency authority in any rolling twelve-month period, regardless of how causes are divided, renamed, sequenced, or characterized.
5. The Tribunal determines whether asserted causes are genuinely distinct.
6. The absolute cap may not be evaded through organizational restructuring, declaration by another officer, relabeling, sequential emergency declarations, or movement of a capability to another entity under Aureus control.

## Article 12 — No Benefit From Constitutional Delay

No actor may obtain through delay, obstruction, nonimplementation, procedural default, failure to constitute a required body, refusal to appoint, failure to fund, or other constitutional breach a substantive advantage the Constitution would deny if the required process had occurred on time.

**The party benefiting from delay shall not benefit from delay.**

Where a required counterpower deadline is missed, Aureus may continue essential existing services but shall not use the period of noncompliance to introduce new high-consequence capabilities, materially increase centralized authority, enter a new high-consequence domain, exceed the population threshold that triggered the body, materially expand geography where that counterpower was required, or materially accelerate institutional growth for the purpose or effect of benefiting from the missing counterpower.

The Tribunal may impose a proportionate enrollment, revenue, authority, capability, or expansion limitation necessary to remove the benefit of unconstitutional delay.

Genuinely urgent assistance to a person is not barred by an institutional growth restriction. An urgent-need exception is recorded and may not become a disguised expansion program.

Obstruction of constitutionally required appointments, funding, elections, sortition, or formation is itself a constitutional violation.

## Article 13 — Constitutional Clock

Where the Constitution establishes a founding-period deadline, the clock begins on the earliest applicable constitutional event, including constitutional ratification, first external member served, first commercial revenue, or first external capital or equivalent financing received.

No actor may defer an obligation merely by delaying formal ratification, organizational classification, or bookkeeping recognition of an operational event.

## Article 14 — Counterpower Funding

1. Once the applicable expenditure threshold is reached, protected annual counterpower funding equals the greater of two percent of the constitutionally defined trailing three-year average of annual operating expenditure, adjusted appropriately for real purchasing power, or the independently assessed amount reasonably necessary for constitutional bodies collectively to perform their duties.
2. A younger institution uses available operating history and a reasonable annualized basis.
3. Protected constitutional capacity shall not automatically shrink merely because current-year institutional spending declines. A reduction in real constitutional capacity requires heightened review.
4. Before the threshold, every counterpower shall possess the minimum viable capability necessary for actual duties, including independence, quorum, competent decision capacity, administrative support, secure records, evidence access, independent advice, complaint or petition intake, urgent-action capability where required, protected publication, and sufficient actual resources.
5. A nominal body incapable of discharging duties is not a constituted counterpower.
6. Operations shall not unilaterally determine what its constitutional reviewers require to function. Funding need is independently assessed. Counterpowers have standing to challenge insufficiency.
7. A funding dispute concerning the Tribunal itself shall use an independent temporary adjudication mechanism rather than final self-determination by the financially interested Tribunal.

## Article 15 — Necessary Service Access and Steward Confidentiality

Access to identifiable Steward communications under a necessary service function is permitted only when reasonably required to deliver, secure, correct, or accountably support the specific assistance the person sought.

Such access is purpose-limited, minimized, authorized, logged, reviewable, subject to confidentiality, and visible through the constitutionally required access record except for a narrowly lawful temporary restriction.

Convenience, product curiosity, generalized analytics, management interest, model improvement, commercial optimization, employee training, or institutional usefulness does not by itself constitute a necessary service function.

Identifiable Steward communications shall not be used for model training or materially equivalent generalized learning without affirmative, specific, revocable opt-in consent.

## Article 16 — Institutional Incentives Are Governed Systems

Aureus shall not evade constitutional prohibitions on engagement optimization by placing the objective in human compensation, team goals, management scorecards, sales targets, promotion criteria, vendor contracts, or another institutional incentive rather than in software.

No person, team, vendor, model, system, or materially member-impacting process shall be rewarded for increasing time spent with Aureus, compulsive return, avoidable dependence, emotional attachment to Aureus, unnecessary message count, retention for its own sake, preventable difficulty leaving, or another metric whose success requires keeping a person engaged beyond that person's interest.

Diagnostic measurement is permitted where it is not converted into a behavioral target. Outcomes measurement shall be governed separately from product incentives.

## Article 17 — Member-Created Value

Where Aureus materially benefits from a member's labor, creative contribution, data contribution beyond ordinary service administration, testing contribution, production work, commercial participation, or another contribution creating material institutional or economic value, Aureus shall determine the appropriate compensation or benefit-sharing under published rules.

Above the constitutional materiality threshold, compensation or meaningful benefit-sharing is presumed unless an independent determination establishes why it is inappropriate.

A member may challenge the determination through the Member Advocate and, where necessary, the Tribunal. Disclosure alone does not satisfy the duty.

## Article 18 — Abundance Measurement

Constitutional abundance review shall consider total central net assets under Aureus's control and entities it controls, including cash, investments, real property, equity interests, subsidiaries, materially realizable intellectual property, mission funds under effective central control, and comparable accumulated wealth, net of genuine liabilities and legally or constitutionally committed obligations.

Artificial restriction, intercompany transfer, subsidiary placement, or accounting classification may not be used to evade an abundance trigger.

Where central net assets exceed the constitutionally established multiple of annual Service Floor cost, the abundance-review duty arises.

## Article 19 — Independent External Critic

The independent external critic shall not depend on the favor of annual operating leadership.

Its funding shall be secured through a constitutionally protected, independently administered, multi-year arrangement sufficient to permit criticism without financial retaliation. Funding may be periodically re-tendered among qualified independent critics.

Loss or reduction of funding may not be based on criticism of Aureus, findings adverse to leadership, refusal to soften a report, public disagreement, or lawful performance of the critic's mandate. A materially adverse funding action following protected criticism carries a rebuttable presumption of retaliation.

## Article 20 — Enforcement Follows Authority

Constitutional enforcement is scoped according to the authority exercised, not merely the organizational chart.

A person or body may not narrow the consequence of a constitutional violation by assigning conduct to a subsidiary, project, unit, contractor, temporary team, special-purpose entity, or newly created reporting line.

Where the responsible actor exercises institution-wide authority, the constitutional remedy may apply institution-wide. Where the violation is local, the remedy should ordinarily remain proportionate to the affected authority.

## Article 21 — Review Quality Over Ritual Frequency

High-consequence capabilities remain leases of authority rather than permanent entitlements.

Longer renewal periods may be authorized for a mature high-consequence capability where multiple independent review cycles are clean, drills succeed, evidence quality is stable, material assumptions have not changed, continuous monitoring is strong, revocation remains rapid, no material incident is unresolved, and the responsible constitutional reviewer affirmatively finds that a longer interval improves rather than weakens oversight.

No extension creates permanent authority. Material change, serious incident, evidence-integrity failure, or changed constitutional assumption reopens authorization regardless of the calendar.

## Article 22 — Costed Service Floor Decisions

A proposal to materially increase or reduce the constitutional Service Floor shall include a costed statement, reasonably conservative demand assumptions, an independent solvency assessment, the expected effect on existing constitutional obligations, alternatives considered, and consequences of error.

The Assembly does not possess authority to create a knowingly unfunded constitutional promise merely by vote. Operations does not possess authority to defeat a lawful Floor merely by asserting cost.

An unresolved conflict between a lawful Floor decision and institutional solvency is a constitutional question for the Tribunal.

Aureus shall reduce scope, redesign delivery, transfer responsibilities, contract responsibly, or leave a domain before knowingly sustaining a structurally false promise.

## Article 23 — Dependency and Meaningful Exit

Structural dependency review begins where leaving Aureus would commonly cause a person to lose two or more essential life functions, or where other evidence indicates equivalent structural dependency.

The Tribunal may initiate qualitative review before mature outcomes data exists.

Relevant evidence includes inability to obtain substitute services, loss of essential records or continuity, financial dependence, housing dependence, health-access dependence, employment dependence, communication dependence, community dependence where institutional control has practical coercive effect, accumulated exit friction, or another condition making nominal exit materially different from meaningful exit.

Aureus shall not wait for statistical certainty before investigating credible dependency.

## Article 24 — Mission Continuity Through Transfer and Jurisdiction

No acquisition, merger, reorganization, insolvency transaction, asset sale, hostile transfer, governmental action, or jurisdictional change may silently extinguish constitutional mission protections attached to mission-critical assets.

Where legally possible, mission-lock covenants bind transferees; constitutional custody arrangements survive restructuring; transaction documents expressly preserve applicable restrictions; and mission-critical rights are not transferred to an entity structurally incapable of honoring them.

Aureus shall diversify mission-protection structures across jurisdictions where concentration in one jurisdiction creates an unreasonable single point of legal failure.

The institution periodically reviews governing law, creditor exposure, enforceability, mission-protection structures, data and credential custody, and jurisdictional concentration risk.

Where law makes a constitutional promise impossible, Aureus shall disclose the conflict and seek lawful restructuring rather than describe an unenforceable aspiration as legal protection.

## Article 25 — Omissions and Non-Decisions

Aureus may violate a constitutional duty by action or by material omission. Absence of a formal decision does not eliminate accountability.

Review may reach an unanswered request, unprocessed application, missed deadline, unexplained disappearance from service, failure to contact a person, failure to escalate known risk, silent eligibility exclusion, failure to provide required notice, failure to transfer or close work, failure to provide a constitutionally required accommodation, and comparable non-decisions.

Where Aureus has undertaken responsibility for a matter, responsibility continues until the work is completed, the member ends it, Aureus lawfully and truthfully closes it, or an accountable handoff is accepted.

A queue is not a constitutional defense.

## Article 26 — Constitutional Evidence Integrity

A constitutional reviewer shall not be required to trust the unsupported self-report of the system, team, model, service, or officer being reviewed.

Material constitutional evidence uses independence proportionate to consequence and, where technically applicable, tamper-evident records, independent logging, separation of duties, authenticated provenance, protected retention, access records, version history, independent sampling, external or cross-system verification, discrepancy reporting, and the ability to distinguish missing evidence from evidence of absence.

A material evidence-integrity failure is itself a constitutional event.

Where the evidence substrate may be compromised, authorization depending on it is treated as uncertain until independently restored.

No body may satisfy an audit solely by generating the evidence that proves its own compliance where an independent source is reasonably available.

## Article 27 — Protected Dissent

A person making a good-faith constitutional complaint, dissent, report, petition, testimony, audit contribution, safety escalation, whistleblowing disclosure, or protected refusal receives constitutional protection from retaliation.

For twenty-four months following the protected act, a materially adverse institutional action materially connected in time or subject matter carries a rebuttable presumption requiring Aureus to establish a legitimate independent basis.

Expiration of the presumption does not legalize retaliation or extinguish an otherwise valid claim.

No confidentiality, loyalty, employment, vendor, volunteer, membership, or settlement arrangement may prohibit protected constitutional reporting.

## Article 28 — Constitutional Integrity Register

Aureus shall maintain and publish a Constitutional Integrity Register.

For each material constitutional power, right, duty, counterpower, trigger, exception, deadline, sanction, review, and remedy, the Register identifies where applicable:

**Authority → Trigger → Actor → Constraint → Independent Reviewer → Remedy → Deadline → Implementing Rule → Required Evidence.**

The Register identifies unresolved delegations, orphan powers, duties without responsible actors, rights without remedies, remedies without enforcement authority, reviews without evidence access, deadlines without consequences, sanctions incapable of reaching the benefit of violation, conflicting definitions, circular references, and dependencies on absent law.

An updated integrity review is required before a material constitutional amendment becomes final. The Register is an interpretive and verification artifact and may not silently amend the Constitution.

## Article 29 — Constitutional Cross-Reference Map

Every constitutional phrase delegating to another source, including "established by," "defined by," "as provided in," "designated by," "according to published law," "heightened constitutional process," and materially equivalent language, shall appear in a maintained cross-reference map.

Each delegation is marked resolved, provisionally resolved, intentionally deferred, or unresolved.

An unresolved delegation affecting an operative right, remedy, emergency power, amendment process, or counterpower is constitutional debt requiring explicit disposition.

## Article 30 — Plain-Language Constitutional Companion

Aureus shall maintain a plain-language constitutional companion explaining what the Constitution means to a person receiving help.

The companion shall be understandable without institutional expertise; explain rights, duties, complaints, remedies, privacy, exit, appeal, and emergency protections; be accessible; be translated into languages materially used by the people Aureus serves as capacity and need require; identify how to obtain human help; and identify where the legal Constitution controls.

The companion is a constitutionally recognized interpretive aid, not an independent amendment instrument. A material divergence between the companion and Constitution is a constitutional defect to be corrected.

No person loses a constitutional right merely because they failed to understand technical constitutional language.

## Article 31 — Plural Constitutional Adversaries

Before mature constitutional closure, Aureus shall obtain independent adversarial review from materially different perspectives, including at minimum:

1. a practitioner with substantial experience serving people materially similar to those Aureus intends to serve, informed where practicable by direct lived-experience review;
2. qualified legal counsel competent in materially relevant jurisdictions and organizational structures;
3. an independent security or safety reviewer specifically attacking evidence integrity, credential custody, authorization, logging, revocation, compromised infrastructure, and false system reporting; and
4. an independent critic whose mandate expressly permits opposition to the premise that Aureus should possess the contemplated degree of influence over people's lives.

Reviewers should receive the Constitution before earlier red-team reports where practical, so their first analysis is not anchored to previous failure models.

Aureus shall preserve findings, its response, whether each finding was accepted, partly accepted, or rejected, the reasons, remaining assumptions, and reconsideration conditions, subject only to legitimate confidentiality and safety limits.

The purpose of adversarial review is not unanimous approval. Disagreement is evidence.

## Article 32 — Founding Review and Dissent Record

Predicted risks shall not be misrepresented as actual institutional failures.

A permanent Founding Review and Dissent Record shall preserve:

**Review → Finding → Response → Disposition → Reasons → Assumptions → Reconsideration Trigger.**

Actual institutional failures belong in the Canon of Failure or its constitutional successor. Warnings and failures are related but distinct evidence.

## Article 33 — Schedule Zero Operative Defaults

Until replaced through a constitutionally valid implementing schedule that is equal or stronger, the following defaults govern:

1. Where a constitutional provision requires approval from another independent body designated by implementing law, the Member Assembly is the designated body unless the Constitution expressly names another.
2. Mission Protector authority is exhaustively governed by Article 6.
3. Tribunal removal follows Article 7.
4. Significant amendment follows Articles 4 and 5.
5. Member initiative follows Article 5.
6. Safeguarding follows Article 9 from the first day a covered person may be served.
7. Protected-dissent presumption lasts twenty-four months.
8. Class A and Class B emergency modification follows Article 10.
9. Emergency duration follows Article 11.
10. Counterpower funding follows Article 14.
11. Structural dependency review begins at two essential life functions or equivalent credible dependency.
12. Abundance review measures total central net assets under institutional control, net of genuine liabilities and committed obligations.
13. Sortition participation follows Article 8.
14. External critic funding is secured on a multi-year basis outside ordinary annual leadership discretion before appointment.
15. Until an independently reviewed durable Assembly population threshold is adopted, a constitutionally constituted Member Assembly is mandatory no later than 2,500 active members; Aureus may constitute it earlier.

## Article 34 — Substance Over Form

No constitutional duty, limitation, threshold, review, funding rule, prohibition, or remedy may be evaded by changing entity, title, department, legal form, accounting classification, vendor relationship, jurisdiction, technical implementation, data location, contract, timing, sequence, or terminology where the substance of the governed conduct remains materially the same.

The Constitution governs functional reality.

## Article 35 — Competitive Pressure Is Not Constitutional Permission

Competitive pressure, profitability, fundraising need, industry practice, technological fashion, customer demand, political pressure, or fear that another institution will act less responsibly does not create constitutional permission.

Where Aureus cannot operate within its constitutional obligations, it shall reduce scope, redesign, transfer responsibly, suspend, or leave the domain.

The Constitution is not suspended by the price of obeying it.

## Article 36 — Founder-Reserved Matters Excluded

Nothing in this document:

1. ends the founding period;
2. invokes OAS-009 Article 16;
3. transfers the Founder's canonizing authority to the Council;
4. terminates, narrows, or expands a Founder veto;
5. determines the Founder's post-transition office;
6. determines Founder compensation, ownership, distributions, benefits, or economic participation;
7. requires surrender of Founder credentials or property; or
8. ratifies a final Founder succession mechanism.

Those matters are expressly reserved for separate Founder reading and decision.

Reservation does not itself create permanent Founder sovereignty and shall not be interpreted as consent to any later transition mechanism.

## 4. Expected constitutional impact

If canonized, this CAP will:

- close the unresolved institutional delegations identified in the third adversarial pass;
- prevent emergency authority from weakening the machinery that detects and reviews emergency conduct;
- make unconstitutional delay costly to the actor benefiting from it;
- make constitutional review capable of reaching omissions and false or compromised evidence;
- prevent engagement incentives from migrating from software into compensation and performance management;
- make counterpowers operationally real rather than nominal;
- establish a durable mechanical integrity layer for future amendments; and
- give the project a single constitutional conformance target while the full consolidated edition is prepared.

It does not end the founding period and does not decide any reserved Founder-personal matter.

## 5. Risks and tradeoffs

1. **Procedural load.** Stronger review can become ceremonial if badly implemented. Article 21 therefore protects review quality over ritual frequency.
2. **Counterpower cost.** Independence costs money. Article 14 permits reduction only through visible constitutional process rather than silent arithmetic decline.
3. **Emergency rigidity.** Class A protection can reduce operational flexibility. The narrow Tribunal-only twenty-four-hour exception exists for cases where stopping a capability would create greater immediate harm.
4. **Growth constraints.** Anti-delay rules can limit enrollment or expansion if required counterpowers are not constituted. Urgent aid remains permitted.
5. **Unsettled thresholds.** The 2,500-member Assembly trigger is explicitly provisional pending independent review.
6. **Legal enforceability.** Mission-lock, transferee, trust, creditor, privacy, employment, and similar external-law effects require competent jurisdiction-specific legal implementation; constitutional text cannot manufacture external enforceability by declaration.
7. **Founder reservation.** The Constitution remains incomplete as to the Founder's final transition until the Founder separately reads and decides those provisions.
