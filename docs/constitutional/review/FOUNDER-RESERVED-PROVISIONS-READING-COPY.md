# Founder-Reserved Constitutional Provisions — Reading Copy

**STATUS: NON-OPERATIVE • NOT A CAP • NOT CANON • NOT RATIFIED**  
**Purpose:** Preserve the subjects intentionally excluded from CAP-015 so the Founder can read and decide them later without losing the work or allowing them to take effect by implication.

---

## Governing rule for this file

Nothing in this file changes the Constitution, ends the founding period, transfers authority, creates an entitlement, changes compensation, changes ownership, requires credential surrender, or binds the Founder to a later decision.

Silence, repository presence, engineering use, citation, or passage of time cannot convert this reading copy into law.

Any operative adoption requires a separate constitutional proposal and the process then required by the Constitution.

---

# I. Founding Act and Institutional Legitimacy

### Candidate principle

The Constitution may acknowledge that the initial founding act was necessarily exercised by the Founder before mature constitutional bodies existed.

A future provision could state that:

1. the Founder's initial constituent act was a bootstrap act necessary to establish the constitutional order;
2. it is not precedent for permanent unilateral sovereignty;
3. it does not manufacture retroactive consent from people who were not present;
4. later institutional affirmation may evidence acceptance of the constitutional order but cannot truthfully be described as retroactive consent from every governed person; and
5. failure to complete a required later affirmation may trigger transparency and anti-delay consequences without automatically voiding rights and protections already in force.

### Question for later Founder decision

Whether Aureus should require a later institutional affirmation of the consolidated Constitution, and if so by which mature bodies, on what timetable, and with what consequence if affirmation is withheld.

---

# II. End of the Founding Period

### Current governing baseline

OAS-009 Article 16 presently provides that the founding period ends only when the Founder approves a Constitutional Amendment Proposal expressly declaring it ended.

### Candidate future principle

A future Founder-approved transition CAP could establish objective readiness conditions showing that Aureus can remain safe, mission-faithful, financially viable, technically governed, and constitutionally accountable without Founder dependence.

Possible evidence could include:

- functioning Member Assembly;
- functioning independent Constitutional Tribunal;
- functioning Mission Protectors;
- tested succession and emergency continuity;
- independent audit and evidence access;
- constitutional funding actually operating;
- mature Library/Foundry/V1 conformance;
- demonstrated ability to detect, report, and remedy constitutional violations without Founder intervention; and
- a tested succession period in which ordinary operations do not require the Founder as a single point of failure.

This reading copy does not select the threshold or end date.

# III. Founder Constitutional Authority After Transition

Possible future models for Founder consideration include:

### Model A — Full constitutional retirement

The Founder ceases to possess special constitutional authority after transition and may continue only in an ordinary office held under the same law as others.

### Model B — Limited mission-guardian role

The Founder retains only a narrowly enumerated mission-protection role with no general executive, amendment, appointment, or operational authority.

### Model C — Time-limited transition office

The Founder retains defined transitional authority for a stated period after the founding period ends, after which it automatically expires.

### Model D — No office required

The Founder may leave formal service entirely while retaining ordinary lawful economic interests, reputation, authorship history, and the ability to participate as any other person may.

No model is selected by this file.

# IV. Founder Veto

A later transition provision must determine separately:

- whether any Founder veto exists at the time of transition;
- which subjects, if any, it reaches;
- whether it expires immediately, phases out, or converts into a narrow mission-protection concurrence;
- whether it can be overridden and by whom;
- whether non-use can create precedent; and
- how any emergency interaction is handled.

No current veto is surrendered, enlarged, or defined by this reading copy.

---

# V. Founder Succession and Post-Transition Service

A future provision may distinguish **constitutional power** from **continued service**.

Ending special Founder authority need not prohibit the Founder from later serving Aureus in an appropriate role, being hired or elected under ordinary rules, advising the institution, founding another mission-aligned institution, or leaving Aureus entirely.

Any continuing office should have written scope, ordinary accountability, conflict rules, and a removal or end-of-service process appropriate to that office.

No continuing office is created here.

---

# VI. Founder Compensation and Economic Participation

A future constitutional provision should distinguish governance sovereignty from lawful economic participation.

Possible protected principles for later review:

1. relinquishing permanent special constitutional authority does not require uncompensated labor;
2. the Founder may receive fair salary, benefits, reimbursement, performance-based compensation, lawful distributions, equity or equivalent economic interests where appropriate to the entity structure, and other legitimate compensation;
3. compensation may not purchase constitutional authority;
4. compensation should be transparent and independently governed when conflicts are material;
5. no compensation arrangement may defeat the Mission, constitutional funding, member rights, solvency, or fiduciary duties; and
6. constitutional transition should not be designed as economic confiscation.

No amount, ownership percentage, distribution right, vesting term, or compensation formula is adopted by this reading copy.

# VII. Founder Property, Credentials, and Constitutional Custody

A future transition instrument may need to distinguish:

- personal property;
- company property;
- intellectual property;
- constitutional credentials;
- production credentials;
- mission-critical keys;
- domain and infrastructure control;
- banking and treasury authority;
- repository ownership and administrative access; and
- emergency recovery authority.

The principle for later drafting should be that mission-critical authority must not remain dependent on one person's private custody after transition, while lawful personal property is not taken merely because the person founded Aureus.

No credential, account, key, property, or right is transferred by this reading copy.

---

# VIII. Founder Economic and Governance Conflicts

If the Founder retains a material economic interest after special constitutional authority ends, a later provision may require independent treatment of transactions in which that interest conflicts with Aureus's duties.

Possible mechanisms include disclosure, recusal, independent valuation, approval by disinterested bodies, publication above materiality thresholds, and Tribunal review for constitutional conflicts.

The purpose would be to allow legitimate economic participation without converting it into hidden constitutional control.

---

# IX. Founder Transition Cannot Be Inferred

A final operative transition provision should likely state expressly that Founder authority does not expire by implication, inactivity, informal delegation, illness, absence, repository change, employment status, title change, public statement, or passage of time.

The transition occurs only through the constitutionally specified act.

Likewise, temporary delegation should not silently become permanent surrender.

---

# X. Founder Transition Should Not Weaken Aureus During the Handoff

A future transition plan should include:

- staged authority transfer where needed;
- credential and custody drills;
- shadow decision periods;
- succession testing;
- documented rollback for technical transfer failures without restoring permanent personal sovereignty;
- member-service continuity;
- financial authority continuity;
- emergency decision continuity;
- public explanation of what changed and what did not; and
- independent certification that the receiving bodies are actually capable of exercising the authority transferred.

---

# XI. Candidate Founding-Affirmation Provision

If the Founder later chooses to include a mature-body affirmation step, one candidate structure is:

1. Founder enactment is acknowledged as the original bootstrap act.
2. After both the Member Assembly and Constitutional Tribunal are operational and independently functional, each conducts a one-time affirmation review within a defined period.
3. The review may affirm the Constitution or propose changes through the ordinary amendment process; it may not silently rewrite it.
4. Non-affirmation does not erase existing rights or dissolve the Constitution by default.
5. Failure to hold the review is recorded publicly and triggers applicable anti-delay consequences.
6. The record states expressly that institutional affirmation is not retroactive individual consent.

This is a candidate only.

---

# XII. Questions the Founder Should Answer Before Any Transition CAP

1. What objective evidence would make you believe Aureus no longer depends on you to remain safe and faithful?
2. Do you want any special role after that point, or would you prefer the freedom to serve only if an ordinary role makes sense?
3. Should any narrow mission-protection authority survive transition, or should every special power end?
4. What economic interests should remain legitimate after governance transition?
5. What property or intellectual-property interests are personal, and what must belong to Aureus or constitutional custody?
6. What transition period is long enough to prove succession without becoming an excuse to postpone it?
7. What event should count as successful handoff?
8. What failure during handoff permits temporary operational rollback, and what failure should instead stop the transfer until corrected?
9. Who independently certifies readiness?
10. What must never be taken from you merely because you gave up special governance authority?
