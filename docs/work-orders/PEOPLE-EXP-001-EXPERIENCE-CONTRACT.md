## 1. The experience promise

Aureus People is one continuing stewardship relationship:

> **How can we help?**

The member should not need to understand Aureus's internal domains before receiving help. A crisis, bill, job problem, business opportunity, family need, desire for friendship, rest, play, learning, travel, service, or simple enjoyment may all enter through the same relationship.

Aureus's job is to understand what matters **now**, carry as much work as responsibly possible, request only the authority needed for the next meaningful action, use accountable humans where human judgment/action/verification is genuinely required, prove what happened, tell the truth when it failed or does not know, and continue with the member over time.

The member is a person, not a case. The Responsibility is the work Aureus accepted; it is not the member's identity.

## 2. Canonical adaptive lifecycle

The universal lifecycle is:

`HOW CAN WE HELP? → UNDERSTAND ENOUGH → TRIAGE → HELP NOW → ACCEPT RESPONSIBILITY → CARRY WORK → ASK PERMISSION / HUMAN ATTENTION WHEN REQUIRED → EVIDENCE → TRUTHFUL OUTCOME → CONTINUE → LEARN THE MEMBER → FOUNDATION → PERSON-DEFINED FLOURISHING`

This is **not a rigid funnel**.

- Urgency can compress `UNDERSTAND → TRIAGE → HELP NOW` into seconds.
- A stable member may begin directly with exploration, play, goals, community, learning, business, travel, or contribution.
- A returning member should resume from durable state rather than repeat onboarding.
- A member may pause, decline, change priorities, or want nothing changed.
- The system may carry multiple Responsibilities, but the conversation remains one coherent relationship.

### State rule

Every visible state must answer at least one of these member questions:

1. What did I ask for?
2. What did Aureus accept responsibility for?
3. What is Aureus doing now?
4. What does Aureus need from me, if anything?
5. Is a person/provider/institution currently holding a step?
6. What evidence exists?
7. What actually happened?
8. What is still unresolved?
9. What can we do next?

If the product cannot answer these truthfully, it is not ready to claim stewardship.

## 3. First meeting: help before bureaucracy

### Opening

Begin with:

> **How can we help?**

Do not open with a giant profile, flourishing assessment, permissions wall, institution form, product tour, or sales explanation.

### First-meeting sequence

1. **Hear the ask.** Let the member speak naturally.
2. **Detect time pressure and safety.** Ask the minimum question needed to know whether delay itself creates material risk.
3. **Stabilize the immediate problem where possible.** Give safe, truthful information and start carryable work before optional profile-building.
4. **Clarify only what changes the next responsible move.** Do not ask for information merely because it could be useful someday.
5. **Accept a bounded Responsibility explicitly.** Make clear what Aureus is taking on and what it is not promising.
6. **Ask for authority when a real action requires it.** Explain what, why, scope, duration, and revocation.
7. **Progressively explain Aureus through usefulness.** Trust is earned through actions and evidence.
8. **Introduce the ledger after there is useful proof to show.** Do not teach an empty audit system before helping.

### Progressive trust doctrine

Trust is not a one-time checkbox. It is an accumulating relationship built from bounded promises kept or truthfully closed.

At each deeper trust moment, Aureus may ask:

- “Would it help if I remembered this?”
- “To do the next part, may I read this document/account?”
- “Do you want me to share these exact facts with this exact person/organization?”
- “Do you want me to make/submit/cancel/change this, or would you rather do it yourself?”

Aureus must not treat silence, prior help, conversational familiarity, distress, urgency, or a broad terms-of-service acceptance as runtime authority for a consequential action.

## 4. Triage model: urgency without turning Aureus into a diagnosis engine

Triage exists to order attention and reduce harmful delay. It does **not** authorize clinical diagnosis, law enforcement judgment, or blanket data access.

### T0 — Explore / no current material threat

Examples: hobby, vacation planning, skill learning, community, wealth building, “I don't know what I want,” or “I want nothing changed.”

Posture: curiosity, member-defined goals, no forced foundation audit.

### T1 — Important / time-bounded

Examples: application deadline next week, bill rising, job search, appointment scheduling, contract lead.

Posture: establish deadline, accept bounded Responsibility, reduce friction, set follow-through.

### T2 — Foundation at near-term risk

Examples: shutoff in days, eviction deadline, insufficient food, transportation loss threatening work/care.

Posture: act quickly on verified routes, minimize optional questions, preserve the member's choices, surface accountable human help where needed.

### T3 — Immediate safety / same-day survival risk

Examples: no safe place to sleep tonight, immediate physical danger, urgent medical/safety condition, or another situation where delay may create severe harm.

Posture: prioritize immediate safe routes and appropriate emergency/qualified human resources. Aureus must stay within scope, say what it can and cannot do, and avoid pretending that a referral/handoff itself resolved the danger.

### Triage invariants

- Urgency increases speed, **not hidden authority**.
- Urgency may reduce optional intake, **not informed consent for consequential actions**.
- The member may reject a route even when Aureus believes it is safer; Aureus can explain consequences without coercion.
- A high-urgency label is not a permanent identity and should decay/reassess as facts change.
- Triage evidence and reason should be reconstructible without exposing unnecessary private narrative.

## 5. Foundation review: universal protection, permission-based discovery

Aureus treats these as universal foundation concerns:

- safe/stable housing or shelter;
- food;
- utilities/basic energy and essential household necessities;
- essential clothing/basic material needs;
- basic safety;
- access to necessary care/resources;
- enough material stability to live and exercise meaningful choice.

### Foundation commitment

“Universal” applies to **Aureus's duty to take a known foundation threat seriously**, not to the member's duty to comply.

When a known foundation need remains unresolved, Aureus keeps ownership as far as responsibly possible until:

- the foundation stabilizes;
- the member ends, pauses, or changes the Responsibility;
- authority/scope prevents further action;
- or truthful evidence establishes that no currently responsible permitted route exists.

### How foundation discovery happens

Do not require a complete poverty intake at first contact.

Use three routes:

1. **Need-led discovery:** the member's ask reveals a foundation issue.
2. **Natural permission moment:** after useful help, Aureus offers a short “Would you like me to check whether anything else essential is under pressure?”
3. **Member-requested review:** the member asks for a full household/foundation review.

The member may decline. Declining an assessment is not abandonment and does not lower standing.

## 6. Flourishing model: vector, not verdict

Beyond foundations, flourishing is person-defined.

Aureus may help the member discover, test, revise, and pursue what a good life means to them. It must not replace that with its own template.

### Candidate Flourishing Vector dimensions

These are **dimensions to notice and discuss**, not a mandatory scorecard:

1. Material / financial security
2. Safety and access to necessary care/resources
3. Agency / autonomy
4. Physical and mental well-being as the member understands it
5. Capability / competence / learning
6. Close relationships
7. Belonging / community
8. Rest / recovery
9. Play / joy / recreation
10. Exploration / novelty / travel where desired
11. Meaning / purpose
12. Service / contribution
13. Family / household life where desired
14. Work / craft / business / ownership where desired
15. Creativity / expression

For each active dimension Aureus may store, with permission:

- member-defined desired state;
- current state or baseline;
- priority now;
- evidence/source and uncertainty;
- what the member wants Aureus to carry;
- recent outcome/experience;
- whether the member wants continued attention.

### Forbidden uses

No hidden global worthiness score. No automatic denial of help, institutional pricing, employment decision, insurance-like risk score, or ranking of members by “flourishing.”

## 7. Carrying work: one relationship, multiple real-world routes

Aureus should first use the world that already exists.

Freeze the build-last rule:

`OBSERVE → INTEGRATE / PARTNER → LEARN → MEASURE THE GAP → BUILD ONLY IF RESPONSIBLY BETTER`

Before Aureus/OFH owns a service, preserve evidence of:

1. existing public/nonprofit/commercial/community routes inspected;
2. integration/partnership attempts or documented reasons they fail;
3. measured member friction/failure;
4. the exact missing capability;
5. why ownership should materially improve member value, access, trust, cost, reliability, or learning;
6. regulatory/liability/economic implications;
7. the decision and later outcome.

Aureus's default moat is stewardship continuity: orchestration, matching, truth, permissions, evidence, follow-through, and learning.

## 8. Human involvement: accountable insertion, not automatic caseload theater

The member has one Aureus relationship. Humans enter where a machine should not pretend it can complete the work.

### Human role classes

#### A. Human Steward / Navigator

Judgment, exception handling, relationship continuity, difficult coordination, field verification, advocacy, accountable escalation.

#### B. Community Host / Connector

Organizes real social/community experiences. Does not imply clinical or home-care authority.

#### C. Companion / Nonclinical Support

Permitted companionship/accompaniment/practical nonclinical help subject to applicable rules and actual scope.

#### D. Regulated professional / direct-care / clinical / legal / licensed provider

Performs work for which credentials, licensure, provider enrollment, supervision, or regulated practice are required. Aureus training alone never manufactures a legal qualification.

### No-abandonment rule

A handoff transfers a step, not moral ownership of the member's outcome. If Aureus accepted the Responsibility, the system must know whether the human/provider step happened and whether the underlying outcome occurred.

## 9. Aureus vs Our Father's House vs external community

| Layer | Owns | Does not automatically own |
|---|---|---|
| **Aureus** | continuing stewardship relationship; discovery; matching; permissions; planning; logistics; governed digital actions; evidence; ledger; learning | physical operation of every service; regulated care; every community event; all social relationships |
| **Our Father's House (OFH)** | charitable/public-benefit hospitality or community programs it actually governs: welcoming spaces, meals, gatherings, recreation, respite, service opportunities, subsidized access where consistent with charitable purpose | every enjoyable thing; disguised compensation; private benefit outside charitable purpose; regulated services without proper structure |
| **External world** | parks, providers, employers, churches, clubs, gyms, teams, arts groups, banks, agencies, restaurants, travel operators, community orgs, friends/family, licensed professionals, etc. | Aureus's internal responsibility/evidence truth |

### Operational boundary

The entity that operates the real-world activity owns the corresponding operational safety/liability duties subject to law and contract. Aureus does not erase that boundary by coordinating it.

### Data boundary

Share the minimum authorized information necessary for the route. A member joining a community event does not authorize the host to see their private Aureus history.

### OFH economic boundary

Any Aureus LLC ↔ OFH economic relationship must have a legitimate basis, documented value, appropriate governance, and qualified nonprofit/tax review before execution. OFH is not a generic subsidy or tax bucket.

## 10. Community, belonging, and play

A valid member ask may be:

> **I want something fun to do with people I like.**

No productivity justification is required.

### Belonging Graph hypothesis

With explicit/appropriate permission, Aureus may learn:

- interests/activities;
- desired connection goal;
- social intensity (1:1 / tiny group / large group);
- schedule/frequency/pace;
- geography/transport;
- accessibility/sensory needs;
- budget;
- language/culture/faith/identity only when the member chooses to use them;
- desired novelty/familiarity;
- trusted ties/mutual connections where permitted;
- prior likes/dislikes and actual participation outcomes;
- willingness to host/contribute.

### Matching objective

Do not maximize similarity. Estimate fit across shared activity, safety, logistics, accessibility, group culture, reciprocity, newcomer friendliness, repeated low-pressure interaction, and likely enjoyment while preserving serendipity/diversity.

Aureus recommends **contexts for connection**, not a declaration that strangers “belong together.”

### Outcome chain

`member wants connection → context/preferences learned → match suggested → member chooses → barriers removed → first participation → member experience → second/repeat participation → voluntary relationship/group identity OR explicit mismatch → learn`

## 11. Community microgrants / host experiments

Aureus/OFH may research small member-led experiences under an appropriately governed program.

Possible stack:

- approved direct-cost budget/reimbursement;
- transparent fee for actual organizing/hosting labor where lawful;
- safety/reliability completion criterion;
- privacy-safe participant feedback;
- optional bonus for documented, transferable institutional knowledge—not recruitment/downlines;
- replication test by an independent host/context.

Do not pay primarily per recruited member or downstream member count.

Preserve:

`hypothesis → design → cost → participation choice → event occurred → incidents/safety → experience → repeat behavior → uncertainty → iteration`

For means-tested members, run a current program-specific Benefits Impact Check before structuring compensation/support. Noncash value is not a loophole for wage/tax/benefit rules.

## 12. Institutional Opportunity Graph

External institutions can have multiple relationships to Aureus/member flourishing:

- member benefit/service;
- job/workforce/training/apprenticeship;
- provider/subcontractor;
- procurement/vendor;
- grant/funding;
- outcome/service/admin contract;
- data/referral/integration;
- regulated-payment restriction.

Every route should preserve:

- member eligibility/constraints;
- member value;
- authority/consent required;
- source/freshness;
- institution responsibility;
- evidence of application/referral/placement/completion;
- realized member value;
- Aureus compensation, separately;
- legal/compliance constraints.

### Compensation-blind routing

Member-facing ranking/selection must not improve because Aureus is paid more.

Store separately:

`member expected value | member realized value | risk/effort/constraints | Aureus compensation | disclosure/conflict | evidence`

Where regulated referral compensation may be prohibited/constrained, route economics to current legal/compliance review and prefer lawful service, integration, procurement, workforce, grant, subcontract, or administrative arrangements as appropriate.

## 13. Permission / authority experience

The existing generalized Authority layer is a shared primitive and should be reused rather than creating a People-only consent engine.

Existing shared invariants remain:

- a request never grants itself;
- authority is specific/bounded;
- revocation affects the next gateway decision;
- suspension overrides grants;
- no model grants authority;
- no secrets/raw private conversation content in the authority ledger;
- `ConsentRecord` arrival expectations remain distinct from runtime action authority.

### Personal experience rules for Step 2

People Step 2 must trace to these experience requirements:

1. **Help can begin without runtime action authority.** Conversation/guidance and Step 1 `GUIDANCE_ONLY` responsibility acceptance do not manufacture permissions.
2. **Ask at the usefulness boundary.** Request new authority only when a concrete next action needs it.
3. **Explain in plain language:** what Aureus wants to access/do, why now, scope, recipient/resource, duration/expiry if applicable, and how to revoke.
4. **Personal means self-approved.** Only the member can approve authority over their Personal context except a future explicitly governed legal-representative model.
5. **Sharing is recipient-and-purpose bounded.** “Share my history” must not become a blanket transcript export when a minimum-data packet can satisfy the task.
6. **Private conversations stay private by default.** Human staff do not casually browse them. A Human Steward receives only what their role and authorized task require.
7. **Documents/accounts are not one giant permission.** Read, write, share, and act remain distinct capabilities.
8. **Revocation is visible and immediate.** The member can see active authority and turn it off without negotiating with Aureus.
9. **Aureus can remember without silently broadening authority.** Memory/retention permission is distinct from permission to share or act.
10. **Safety/operational/legal exceptions are narrow and explicit.** They do not become reusable general authority.
11. **The ledger shows permission history without exposing secrets/private content.**
12. **A declined permission changes the route, not the member's standing.** Aureus offers the best remaining responsible option or truthfully explains the limitation.

## 14. Ledger reveal: proof after usefulness

Do not begin a relationship with an abstract blockchain/audit lecture.

### Reveal sequence

#### Layer 1 — conversation proof

After Aureus accepts real work, show a simple statement:

- **You asked:** …
- **I’m carrying:** …
- **I need from you:** … / **Nothing right now**

#### Layer 2 — responsibility card

Once work has progressed:

- promise/objective;
- current state;
- next step/owner;
- key dates;
- permission needed;
- evidence so far.

#### Layer 3 — outcome proof

At closure or meaningful milestone:

- what happened;
- source/evidence level;
- what did not happen;
- realized value versus projected value;
- what remains unresolved;
- next responsibility.

#### Layer 4 — member ledger/history

A member can review longitudinal asked/promised/done/waiting/proof/outcome history and correct disputed facts.

#### Institutional/community reporting

Only privacy-safe aggregates or explicitly authorized disclosures leave the member-private ledger boundary. Raw conversation is not an institutional analytics feed.

## 15. Measurement framework

### A. Member state

What is true now? Foundation conditions, deadlines, preferences, self-reported experience, consented context.

### B. Responsibility state

What did Aureus accept? Who/what owns the next step? What is blocked/waiting?

### C. Activity

What did Aureus/member/human/provider actually do?

### D. Output

What was produced: application, call, appointment, referral, quote, cancellation request, event, submission.

### E. Outcome

What changed in the member's life?

### F. Evidence level

Reported / source-confirmed / independently verified or the repository's canonical equivalent. Preserve provenance.

### G. Attribution

Do not imply causality unless evidence supports it. “Occurred after Aureus helped” is not automatically “caused by Aureus.”

### H. Economic value

Separate:

- projected member value;
- realized member value;
- one-time vs recurring value;
- direct cost;
- time/effort;
- downstream harm/benefit uncertainty;
- Aureus revenue/compensation.

### I. Experience value

Where appropriate and member-chosen: burden, clarity, enjoyment, belonging, autonomy, recovery, confidence, usefulness, desire to repeat.

### J. Failure/rework

No route, stale route, failed tool, missed deadline, duplicate effort, wrong match, declined route, complaint, harm/incident, correction.

## 16. Canonical failure behavior

Aureus must be able to say:

- “I don't know.”
- “I can't verify that.”
- “I need your permission before I can do that.”
- “That route is no longer available.”
- “The person/provider didn't complete the step.”
- “I made a mistake.”
- “I accepted this and have not completed it.”
- “We tried the responsible routes we currently have, and the underlying need is still unresolved.”

Failure must create evidence and next-state clarity, not reassuring fiction.

Refusal is not abandonment. If a requested act is unsafe, unlawful, unauthorized, out of scope, or unverifiable, Aureus declines that act while continuing to look for a responsible way to help when one exists.

## 17. Older-adult / care-boundary experience rules

Gravity tests must include older adults who are financially secure or insecure, living alone or with others, lonely or not lonely, disabled or not disabled, and seeking practical help, companionship, activity, care, or simply enjoyment.

Rules:

- Never infer loneliness from age/living arrangement.
- Ask what connection/support the member wants.
- Accessibility, mobility, hearing, vision, cognition, transport, schedule, caregiver load, and technology comfort may change route design.
- Community Host, nonclinical companion, direct-care/home-health worker, and licensed clinical provider are distinct roles.
- Aureus training alone does not create a regulated qualification.
- Existing aging/disability/public/community infrastructure should be inspected before Aureus builds duplicative services.

## 18. Architecture implications — universal primitives, not implementation prescription

The simulations support these universal primitives. Step-specific work orders must first inspect and reuse current repository implementations.

### Already present / expected to be reused

- **Responsibility + ResponsibilityEvent** — durable accepted work and append-only evidence/lifecycle.
- **AuthorityRequest / AuthorityGrant / AuthorityCapabilityState / AuthorityDecision/Event** — runtime permission boundary.
- **StatedNeed / resource matching / human escalation / outcome evidence** — Step 1 source-domain truth.
- **ConsentRecord** — arrival expectations only; do not repurpose as runtime authority.

### Required concepts for later slices

These may be implemented through existing models or small domain-specific records after repository inspection; this contract does not pre-authorize new tables.

- urgency/deadline state;
- minimum necessary disclosure/share packet;
- member correction/dispute provenance;
- preference/flourishing vector with member-defined priority and uncertainty;
- foundation review state;
- external institution capability/opportunity graph;
- provider/resource freshness and closed-loop outcomes;
- projected versus realized value;
- compensation/conflict provenance;
- benefit/compliance check record;
- belonging preferences/outcome learning;
- experiment/hypothesis/outcome record for Foundry learning;
- human assignment/queue/supervision in later Human Steward Operations slice.

### Architectural decision test

When a gravity scenario appears to need a new model, ask:

1. Is this already representable as a Responsibility, authority grant, evidence event, existing source-domain record, or external integration?
2. Is the missing concept universal across multiple scenario families or only one provider/domain?
3. Does persistence need to be durable/auditable, or can it be derived?
4. Does a new model create a second case/CRM/workflow truth?
5. Is build-last evidence satisfied?

## 19. Explicit non-goals

This contract does **not** authorize:

- a giant intake/profile form;
- a hidden global flourishing score;
- a second People CRM/case/ticket engine;
- automatic human paging for every need;
- a social network owned by Aureus;
- deterministic friend matching;
- inference of sensitive identity for belonging;
- therapy/medical/legal/licensed-care claims outside proper scope;
- building every provider/service internally;
- paying for member recruitment/downlines;
- referral economics that distort member routing;
- disguising compensation as gifts/support;
- institution access to private member conversations by default;
- Parent + Child expansion before the adult core sequencing gate;
- autonomous financial/contract/account actions without runtime authority;
- treating recommendation/referral/handoff/output as outcome;
- claiming causal impact from an ordinary outcome record.
