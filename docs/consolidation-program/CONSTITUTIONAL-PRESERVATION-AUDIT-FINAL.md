## Part 1 — The seven items, resolved

### C4. Dignity on exit from the Covenant

**ALC-001 (Art. V §11):** *"A person may voluntarily withdraw from the Stewardship Covenant. Withdrawal from the Covenant does not diminish the inherent dignity or worth of that person."*

**OAS lineage found:** `OAS-008` Art. 16 §1 (Voluntary Resignation): *"Every Member holds the right to resign their membership at any time, for any reason or none... This right may not be denied, conditioned on approval, or made contingent on the System's convenience."* Art. 17 §2 (Suspension) and Art. 18 §2 (Removal), respectively: *"it does not diminish the universal rights a suspended person retains as a Guest under Article 5, which remain unaffected regardless of membership status"* and *"A removed person retains every universal right of OAS-006 as a Guest, and this Article does not diminish them."* `OAS-006` Art. 1 §2 (Right to Dignity) is explicit that this right *"is absolute. It admits no balancing..."*

**Comparison:** OAS-008 doesn't state "leaving doesn't diminish dignity" as a single direct sentence the way ALC-001 does. It gets there by a two-step structural guarantee instead: (1) a departing or removed person automatically retains full Guest status, and (2) Guest status includes the absolute Right to Dignity. The *substance* is fully covered — arguably more robustly, since it's backed by an enforceable right rather than a declared value — but the *directness* is different.

**Classification: Already Represented.**
**Confidence: High.**

### R1. Institutional strength measured by care for the vulnerable

**ALC-001 (Art. IV §11):** *"Those who possess greater authority, knowledge, experience, or capability bear greater responsibility toward those who possess less. Children, vulnerable persons, and those experiencing hardship deserve particular care, patience, dignity, and protection. Institutional strength shall be measured not by how Aureus serves the strongest, but by how faithfully it serves those most in need."*

**OAS lineage found:** Nothing. I read `OAS-006` (Rights, 20 Articles), `OAS-007` (Duties, 23 Articles), `OAS-008` (Membership, 23 Articles), `OAS-009` (Governance, 18 Articles), `OAS-010` (Amendment, 18 Articles), and `OAS-011` (Justice, 18 Articles) in full. None contains a provision addressing children, vulnerable persons, hardship, or a capability-asymmetry duty of care. The nearest adjacent text, `OAS-008` Art. 21 §1 (*"A community that strengthens its Members while discarding those who leave has not fulfilled the Collective's promise"*), addresses a different population (departing members) under a different theory (the Collective's promise), not vulnerability generally.

**Comparison:** A genuine, confirmed gap. Nothing in the OAS lineage protects children, the vulnerable, or those in hardship as a named category, and nothing states a "measure institutional strength by how it treats the weakest, not the strongest" standard.

**Classification: Preserve Verbatim.**
**Confidence: High** — checked against all six directly relevant OAS documents in full, not sampled.

### R2. Interpersonal-tone norms (contempt, restoration, service before recognition)

**ALC-001 (Art. IV §12):** *"Disagreement shall never justify contempt. Correction shall seek restoration before condemnation. Authority shall seek service before recognition."*

**OAS lineage found, by clause:**
- *"Disagreement shall never justify contempt"* — `OAS-007` Art. 8 (Duty to Preserve Space for Disagreement): *"Every Steward owes a duty not to suppress, penalize, or discourage good-faith criticism or disagreement... good-faith dissent is a constitutionally protected form of participation, not a tolerated exception to loyalty."* Protects the *right* to disagree; says nothing about contempt as a prohibited *response* to disagreement.
- *"Correction shall seek restoration before condemnation"* — `OAS-011` Art. 12 (Available Remedies) authorizes the Tribunal to *"order the restoration of a right, status, or standing wrongfully denied."* This is restoration of the *wronged person's* position, a remedy category — not a rehabilitative stance toward *the person found in violation*, which is what ALC-001's clause is about. Different concept wearing the same word.
- *"Authority shall seek service before recognition"* — `OAS-004` Art. 4 (Service): institutional self-perpetuation is subordinated to service generally, but nothing frames this as a personal-conduct norm for individuals holding authority specifically.

**Comparison:** Partial coverage at best, and what coverage exists uses different mechanisms (a right to dissent; a remedy category) rather than the same idea. The specific rehabilitative-orientation-toward-wrongdoers concept ("restoration before condemnation") has no OAS equivalent at all.

**Classification: Preserve in Adapted Form.**
**Confidence: Medium-high** — the "restoration before condemnation" clause is confidently unaddressed; the other two clauses have partial, imperfect analogues, which is why this isn't "Preserve Verbatim."

### G2. The "Not Yet Ready to Decide" participant option

**ALC-001 (Art. VII §7, §8):** Three participation options — *"Approve. Do Not Approve. Not Yet Ready to Decide."* — with: *"Choosing not to decide immediately shall never be treated as failure or disloyalty. Responsible stewardship sometimes requires additional learning."*

**OAS lineage found:** `OAS-010` (the full CAP lifecycle, read in full) has no equivalent at all — and the reason is more significant than a missing option. `OAS-010`'s process is: any person may *submit* a CAP (Art. 3); the CAP is reviewed by the Tribunal for entrenchment conflicts and undergoes *"a period of deliberation... during which the proposal is available for examination by any person"* (Art. 5 §1); and the **Canonizing Authority** (the Founder, later the Council) alone decides to approve, reject, or return it for revision. There is no broad-participant vote anywhere in `OAS-010` — "eligible participant," in the sense ALC-001 Article VII uses it, doesn't exist as a voting concept in the OAS lineage at all.

**Comparison:** This isn't just one missing option — ALC-001 assumes a participatory-vote model (a "constitutional matters" vote open to "eligible participants") that `OAS-010` doesn't have. `OAS-010` is authority-decides-after-open-deliberation, not community-votes. The "Not Yet Ready to Decide" option is a detail of a voting architecture the OAS lineage never built.

**Classification: Preserve in Adapted Form** — but flag explicitly: this is smaller than the real open question, which is whether the unified Constitution wants a participatory-vote amendment model at all (ALC-001's assumption) or an authority-decides model (OAS-010's actual design, and the one 12-of-13 department Charters and the entire operative tree are already built against).
**Confidence: High** on the finding of absence; this is a genuine architectural fork, not a wording gap.

### G3. Non-erasure of constitutional history

**ALC-001 (Art. VII §12, Art. IX §9):** *"Constitutional history shall never be erased."* / *"Historical records shall not be rewritten in a manner that falsely represents what previously occurred... Revision of history weakens it."*

**OAS lineage found:** `OAS-010`, read in full, has an extensive, operative regime on exactly this point:
- Art. 13 §2: *"No Archived text may be deleted, and no Archived text may be cited as current operative law."*
- Art. 15 §1 (Version Preservation): *"Every version of every constitutional document, from its first Draft through every subsequent amendment, shall be preserved in the constitutional record. No version is overwritten or deleted when a new version is canonized; the new version is added, and the record shows the complete sequence."*
- Art. 16 (No Silent Revision): *"No constitutional document may be altered, in whole or in part, except through the process this document establishes. A change made outside this process has no canonical effect, regardless of its source or apparent authority."*

**Comparison:** This is not merely covered — it's covered more rigorously than ALC-001's single-sentence commitment. ALC-001 states a value ("history shall never be erased"); `OAS-010` builds the mechanism (append-only version chain, an Office of the Constitutional Record whose job is enforcing exactly this, and a rule that unauthorized changes are void regardless of who made them).

**Classification: Already Represented**, and materially strengthened.
**Confidence: High.**

### O3. Severability clause

**ALC-001 (Art. XII §4):** *"If any provision of this Constitution is determined to be invalid, unenforceable, or incapable of implementation under applicable law, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force... Aureus shall adopt the narrowest constitutional amendment necessary to restore consistency while preserving the original constitutional intent."*

**OAS lineage found:** Nothing. Checked `OAS-001`, `OAS-004`, `OAS-005` (Constitutional Construction, the natural home for a clause like this), `OAS-006` through `OAS-011` — no severability provision anywhere.

**Classification: Preserve Verbatim** (or near-verbatim) — a standard, low-controversy legal-instrument provision, genuinely absent.
**Confidence: High** — checked against every constitutional-tier OAS document read this session.

### O4. Translation/language-authority clause

**ALC-001 (Art. XII §6):** *"The official constitutional text shall be maintained in English. Translations may be prepared to increase accessibility and understanding. In the event of any material conflict between translations, the officially ratified English text shall govern..."*

**OAS lineage found:** Nothing. Same check as O3 — absent from every document read.

**Classification: Preserve Verbatim** — standard, low-controversy, genuinely absent.
**Confidence: High.**

## Summary

**25 items total** carried forward: 13 Preserve Verbatim (5 of them contingent on a parent item — the Mark, the Covenant — also surviving), 12 Preserve in Adapted Form. Everything else identified across both audits — the substantial majority of `ALC-001`'s operative content (Rights, Governance, Amendment, AI/Technology, the Ledger, Justice, and now confirmed: exit-dignity and history-non-erasure) — is already represented in the OAS lineage, in most cases more thoroughly.

**Two items are flagged as bigger than they look** and deserve explicit Founder attention before drafting begins, not just inclusion on this list: the participatory-vote question (G2) is really a fork in amendment architecture, and the vulnerable-persons gap (R1) is a genuine, previously-undetected protection absence in the operative rights document.

This inventory is what the Founder asked for: confidence that no enduring constitutional idea gets lost in the merge. It is not a draft, a merge, or a recommendation on exact wording — that remains the next, separate step.
