# Aureus Executive Intelligence Standard

**Status:** Proposed shared Foundry / executive operating standard  
**Scope:** Founder’s Office, executive skills, Production House, strategic research, planning, product, operations, finance, technology, security, trust, legal/compliance, member experience, business experience, communications, and future executive domains  
**Principle:** One Aureus institutional intelligence system; many executive skills and lenses; one shared truth base.

## 1. Purpose

Aureus executive work must not become a collection of disconnected AI personas, private prompt traditions, or unequal context windows.

Every executive skill must begin from the same institutional facts, mission constraints, current operating state, evidence standards, terminology, decision history, and authority boundaries. Domain specialization changes the questions asked and the expertise applied; it does not create a separate reality.

The goal is to improve the quality of questions, prompts, plans, creative briefs, decisions, and execution before work begins.

This standard is especially important for the Production House, where weak upstream understanding can create polished but strategically wrong output.

## 2. Executive architecture

The default architecture is:

```text
Founder / authorized decision-maker
        |
        v
Aureus institutional intelligence / Foundry
        |
        +--> CEO / Strategy skill
        +--> Product skill
        +--> Operations skill
        +--> Finance skill
        +--> Technology / Engineering skill
        +--> Security / Privacy / Trust skill
        +--> Legal / Compliance skill
        +--> Member / Customer Experience skill
        +--> Research / Market Intelligence skill
        +--> Communications / Brand skill
        +--> Production House skill
        +--> Red-Team / Critic skill
        +--> Verification skill
```

These are executive **skills / perspectives**, not independent characters competing for control. A worker or autonomous agent may be used internally by a skill when multi-step execution requires one, but authority, memory, provenance, approvals, and final synthesis remain governed by Aureus.

## 3. Shared Executive Context Packet

Before material executive analysis, every participating skill receives the same current `ExecutiveContextPacket`.

Minimum packet:

1. **Mission and constitutional constraints**
   - current mission and non-negotiables;
   - truth, privacy, agency, authorization, safety, ownership, and stewardship boundaries;
   - applicable policy and governance constraints.

2. **Current institutional state**
   - what is live;
   - what is being built;
   - current priorities;
   - known blockers and unresolved questions;
   - current commitments and deadlines;
   - current financial / operational / product condition when relevant.

3. **Decision history**
   - prior decisions relevant to the question;
   - rationale;
   - assumptions made at the time;
   - what has changed since;
   - unresolved dissent.

4. **Evidence state**
   - verified facts;
   - observations;
   - source provenance;
   - confidence / uncertainty;
   - missing evidence;
   - known contradictions.

5. **Outcome state**
   - expected outcomes previously predicted;
   - actual outcomes;
   - prediction gaps;
   - failures, friction, and successful patterns;
   - lessons already accepted into institutional knowledge.

6. **Decision frame**
   - exact question;
   - desired outcome;
   - affected people / systems;
   - constraints;
   - reversibility;
   - consequence level;
   - decision owner;
   - required approvals.

No executive skill may silently substitute stale or private context for this packet.

## 4. Required intelligence mix

Executive reasoning must draw from a healthy mix of evidence instead of overfitting to one kind of source.

### A. Aureus first-party reality

Highest-value evidence when available:

- member / customer outcomes;
- product telemetry;
- support and incident records;
- sales and pipeline data;
- financial actuals;
- operational cycle times;
- failure and recovery records;
- field observations;
- interviews and direct qualitative evidence;
- forecast-versus-actual records;
- experiments and validation results.

First-party data must be privacy-minimized and used only within valid authority.

### B. Real-world operator evidence

Use evidence from organizations and practitioners that have actually performed comparable work:

- operating playbooks;
- case studies with measurable outcomes;
- postmortems;
- implementation reports;
- public financial / operating disclosures;
- product and growth teardown evidence;
- customer behavior data;
- industry benchmarks;
- practitioner interviews;
- examples of both success and failure.

Study famous or high-performing leaders for observable practices, decision systems, incentives, organizational design, tradeoffs, and outcomes — not for personality imitation or hero worship.

### C. Academic and institutional evidence

Where the problem benefits from established research, use:

- systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
- peer-reviewed research;
- credible working papers when appropriately labeled;
- university and research-institute work;
- government statistics and administrative datasets;
- professional standards;
- standards bodies;
- regulatory and official guidance;
- validated measurement frameworks.

Academic evidence must be checked for population, setting, age, applicability, replication, effect size, and date before being generalized to Aureus.

### D. Creative and adjacent-domain references

Creative work must not be generated from an empty room.

The system should deliberately study relevant and adjacent examples from:

- film and television;
- advertising and brand campaigns;
- documentary and journalism;
- product and interaction design;
- architecture and physical environments;
- games and interactive storytelling;
- publishing and editorial design;
- music and sound design;
- retail and hospitality experiences;
- education and museums;
- live events;
- creator workflows;
- historically important works;
- emerging creative communities.

References are inputs for principles, patterns, contrasts, composition, pacing, emotional structure, production technique, and quality bars. Aureus must not simply imitate another creator’s protected expression.

For every meaningful creative brief, collect both **positive references** (what to learn from) and **counter-references** (what to avoid and why).

### E. Market and competitive intelligence

Maintain a current view of:

- direct competitors;
- adjacent substitutes;
- new entrants;
- platform shifts;
- pricing and packaging;
- distribution changes;
- customer expectations;
- category growth / contraction;
- regulation relevant to the category;
- new workflows that reduce cost or increase quality;
- open-source alternatives;
- acquisition, partnership, and ecosystem movement when material.

Competitor information is evidence, not a command to copy. The question is always: **What changed in the environment, what does it make newly possible or newly dangerous, and does it improve Aureus’s ability to help people flourish?**

### F. Tool and capability intelligence

The executive system and Production House must maintain a living capability map for tools and providers.

Track, where material:

- provider and tool;
- current model / version;
- capability class;
- quality strengths and weaknesses;
- modalities;
- context / memory characteristics;
- tool use / computer use / agentic capability;
- latency;
- reliability;
- API / integration availability;
- pricing and unit economics;
- usage limits;
- privacy / data handling;
- licensing / commercial-use terms;
- safety / policy constraints;
- geographic availability;
- deprecations;
- migration requirements;
- known failure modes;
- evidence from Aureus’s own evaluations.

Do not choose a tool because it is fashionable, because credits exist, or because a vendor claims superiority. Route work based on measured fitness for the task.

## 5. Source hierarchy

Prefer evidence in roughly this order, while allowing domain-specific exceptions:

1. direct Aureus evidence from the actual problem;
2. authoritative primary source or original dataset;
3. official technical / regulatory / financial source;
4. high-quality independent research;
5. credible practitioner evidence;
6. secondary analysis;
7. community sentiment / anecdote as a discovery signal only.

A popular claim does not become true through repetition.

When sources conflict, preserve the conflict and investigate it. Do not average incompatible claims into fake certainty.

## 6. Freshness standard

Every material fact that can change must carry a freshness expectation.

Suggested classes:

- **Live / near-live:** outages, pricing, availability, active incidents, production state.
- **Daily:** major model / tool releases, important competitor moves, security advisories, material regulatory announcements.
- **Weekly:** tool capability map, competitor feature changes, major market signals, material research relevant to active work.
- **Monthly:** category landscape, pricing comparison, workflow benchmarks, strategic assumptions.
- **Quarterly:** deeper market structure, positioning, portfolio, capital allocation, long-range assumptions.
- **Event-triggered:** immediately re-evaluate when a major vendor changes an API, price, model, license, policy, safety profile, or integration contract.

Stale evidence must be labeled stale rather than silently reused.

## 7. Intelligence radar

Foundry should maintain a continuous external intelligence radar with at least these lanes:

1. **AI / model providers** — official release notes, model cards, API docs, pricing, deprecations, safety reports.
2. **Creative production** — image, video, audio, voice, design, editing, 3D, animation, collaboration, asset-management tools.
3. **Enterprise / productivity** — browser, office suite, workflow automation, CRM, communications, data, research, knowledge tools.
4. **Developer ecosystem** — coding systems, frameworks, agent infrastructure, observability, evaluation, security, deployment.
5. **Competitors and substitutes** — product changes, positioning, packaging, partnerships, distribution, customer reaction.
6. **Research** — relevant academic, institutional, evaluation, human-factors, economics, organizational, behavioral, and technical research.
7. **Markets and operations** — category demand, customer behavior, cost curves, labor / workflow changes, regulation, and adoption.
8. **Creative culture** — noteworthy work, formats, storytelling patterns, emerging aesthetics, audience behavior, and new distribution formats.

Items entering the radar are not automatically accepted as institutional truth. They become candidates for evaluation, testing, or incorporation.

## 8. Question and prompt compiler

Before material work, Aureus should compile the request into a better question rather than merely sending the Founder’s first wording directly to a model or worker.

The compiler asks internally:

1. What are we actually trying to accomplish?
2. For whom?
3. What does success look like in observable terms?
4. What facts do we already know?
5. What do we only infer or assume?
6. What important information is missing?
7. What has already been tried?
8. What relevant historical / creative / operator / academic examples exist?
9. What counterexamples or failures matter?
10. What current tools or capabilities change what is possible?
11. What constraints are non-negotiable?
12. What could make the apparent best answer fail in reality?
13. What must be verified before action?
14. What is the smallest responsible test?

The resulting prompt / work order should contain enough context to create good work without burying the worker in irrelevant material.

## 9. Common executive answer format

Unless the task clearly requires another form, material executive recommendations use the same structure:

1. **Decision / question** — what is being decided.
2. **Current reality** — concise shared facts.
3. **What changed** — new evidence, market change, or new capability.
4. **Relevant evidence** — internal, operator, academic/institutional, creative/reference, market/tool evidence as appropriate.
5. **Interpretation** — clearly separated from facts.
6. **Options** — genuinely distinct paths when there is a real choice.
7. **Tradeoffs / risks** — including mission and trust effects.
8. **Domain recommendation** — from that executive skill’s lens, with rationale.
9. **Dissent / counter-case** — strongest credible disagreement.
10. **Unknowns** — what remains unproven.
11. **What would change the recommendation** — disconfirming evidence.
12. **Next responsible test / action** — owner, evidence required, and completion condition.

Facts, inference, judgment, and uncertainty must never be blended into one unlabeled voice.

## 10. Executive synthesis

When multiple executive skills participate, Foundry should not merely vote.

Synthesis should:

- identify genuine agreement;
- identify disagreements and their root assumptions;
- detect whether two skills are optimizing different objectives;
- compare claims against common evidence;
- test important assumptions;
- use a red-team / critic when consequences warrant;
- use a verifier independent from the producer when practical;
- preserve unresolved dissent for the decision owner;
- record the final decision, rationale, expected result, and what evidence would prove it right or wrong.

The Founder should see the smallest useful synthesis, with deeper evidence available on demand.

## 11. Production House preflight

Before Production House creates a material artifact or campaign, compile a `ProductionBrief` containing:

- desired outcome;
- audience;
- job to be done;
- message / meaning;
- factual claims and required sources;
- emotional objective where appropriate;
- brand / institutional constraints;
- medium and distribution environment;
- positive references;
- counter-references;
- relevant audience / market behavior;
- available production tools and why they fit;
- rights / licensing / provenance constraints;
- accessibility requirements;
- budget / time / format constraints;
- variants or experiments worth producing;
- acceptance criteria;
- verification method.

Production House should be able to explain why a creative direction was chosen, not only generate an attractive result.

## 12. Learning loop

Every important decision or produced artifact should be eligible for the same loop:

```text
question / intent
-> compiled context and evidence
-> executive / production work
-> prediction / expected result
-> action or release
-> real-world evidence
-> actual result
-> prediction gap / friction / success
-> Foundry analysis
-> candidate lesson
-> independent validation when material
-> governed Library update
```

This preserves what Aureus learns without turning one success, one failure, one executive opinion, or one model output into permanent doctrine prematurely.

## 13. Minimum anti-patterns

Do not:

- create isolated executive personalities with different private realities;
- imitate famous leaders as personas;
- treat a model’s confidence as evidence;
- use only academic theory without field reality;
- use only anecdotes without broader evidence;
- use only competitor behavior as strategy;
- chase every new tool release;
- keep a tool because Aureus already paid for it;
- let creative work begin without a real brief when the stakes justify one;
- confuse references with permission to copy;
- hide dissent from the Founder;
- present stale market or tool information as current;
- promote external intelligence directly into live policy without evaluation and authority.

## 14. Completion criteria for this standard

This standard is operational when:

1. executive skills consume a shared context packet;
2. a common evidence / provenance schema distinguishes fact, inference, judgment, and uncertainty;
3. Foundry can route the smallest competent mix of executive skills for a question;
4. the external intelligence radar has source, freshness, provenance, and deduplication rules;
5. a living tool / capability map exists;
6. Production House uses the preflight brief for material work;
7. executive outputs follow the common answer contract;
8. decisions record predictions and actual outcomes;
9. lessons flow through Foundry validation into governed Library knowledge;
10. stale intelligence can be detected and refreshed instead of silently reused.

## 15. Governing idea

**Same truth. Different expertise. Better questions. Better work. Measured against reality.**

The executive team exists to increase the quality of Aureus’s judgment and execution, not to multiply voices. Creativity, scholarship, operating experience, market awareness, current technology, dissent, and measured outcomes should meet in one governed intelligence system before consequential work is carried forward.
