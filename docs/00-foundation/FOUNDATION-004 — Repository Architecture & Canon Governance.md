# FOUNDATION-004
# Repository Architecture & Canon Governance

Status: Permanent
Authority: Constitutional

---

# Purpose

Aureus is not only a mission. It is a body of documents that describes and governs how that mission is carried out.

That body of documents must remain trustworthy.

A document family that no one can identify, an identifier that means two different things, a rule that quietly competes with another rule at the same level — each of these weakens trust in the whole record, even where the mission itself remains sound.

This document establishes the permanent rules by which the Aureus repository organizes, names, registers, migrates, merges, retires, and cross-references its own canon.

FOUNDATION-003 governs the order of authority among documents.

This document governs how documents are built, named, and kept honest within that order.

---

# 1. One Family, One Purpose

Every document family — every distinct identifier prefix — has exactly one purpose.

One authority.

One audience.

One relationship to every other family.

No family exists merely because it was historically created.

A family whose purpose has been absorbed by another shall be retired, not left standing beside its replacement.

---

# 2. Single Source of Truth

Every concept within Aureus shall have exactly one authoritative source of truth.

Supporting documents may elaborate, summarize, or reference that source.

They shall not redefine it.

They shall not compete with it.

Where a reader finds two documents each appearing to define the same concept authoritatively, that is not a disagreement to interpret.

It is a defect to resolve.

---

# 3. Authority Flows Downward

Authority flows downward through the levels FOUNDATION-003 establishes.

Never sideways.

Documents at the same level shall not silently compete for authority over the same concept.

Where two same-level documents overlap, that overlap shall be resolved by consolidation or by an explicit, written scope boundary between them.

Silence is never a resolution.

---

# 4. Prefix Governance

Every new document family requires a new, unique identifier prefix.

A prefix shall not be assigned until a search of the full repository confirms it is not already in use.

A prefix, once assigned to a family, belongs to that family alone.

---

# 5. Identifier Uniqueness

No two documents may share the same identifier unless one is a lawful revision of the other.

Where an identifier collision is discovered, it shall be recorded and flagged for resolution.

It shall never be resolved silently by assuming one document controls.

---

# 6. Registry Requirement

Every document family shall be recorded in the repository's registry before its documents are moved, merged, or retired.

A registry entry states a family's purpose, its authority tier, its location, and its status.

Repository changes shall follow the registry.

The registry shall not be written after the fact to describe what was already done.

---

# 7. Deprecation Policy

Every document moves through a defined lifecycle: Draft, Living Draft, Active, Deprecated, Retired, Historical.

A document is Deprecated when its replacement has been identified but migration is not yet complete.

Deprecation is a warning, not a removal. A Deprecated document remains available and may still be cited during the transition, but shall state clearly what is replacing it and shall not be treated as the preferred source for new work.

A document need not pass through every stage. A document retired for a confirmed duplicate, for example, may move directly from Active to Retired. Deprecation exists for the more common case: an orderly transition that takes time to complete.

---

# 8. Retirement Policy

A document may be retired when its purpose has been fully absorbed by another document or family.

A document typically passes through Deprecated status before Retirement, except where immediate retirement is warranted under the preceding Article.

Retirement removes a document from operative use.

Retirement does not erase a document.

A retired document's history remains permanently available through the repository's version history, consistent with the non-erasure principle already established for the Aureus constitutional record.

---

# 9. Redirect Policy

Every retired document shall carry a redirect notice stating what replaced it.

A redirect notice is added to the retired document. It is never a substitute for preserving that document's original text in history.

A reader who encounters a retired document shall never be left wondering where its content went.

---

# 10. Repository Migration Rules

A document may migrate from aspirational status to engineering authority, from one family to another, or from one authority tier to another, only through a recorded decision, never through informal adoption.

This rule governs every kind of repository document, not canon alone. Work orders, product architecture, governance documents, registries, standards, blueprints, experience documents, and steward documents all migrate under the same discipline.

Engineering work shall cite only documents holding engineering authority.

A document does not become authoritative merely by being cited once. It becomes authoritative through a recorded migration decision, entered in the registry.

---

# 11. Merge Policy

Where two documents substantially duplicate one another, the more complete and more internally consistent document survives as the base.

The surviving document shall be reviewed for content unique to the document it is replacing, and that unique content shall be carried forward before the losing document is retired.

A merge that discards unique content without recording what was discarded is not a faithful merge.

---

# 12. Cross-Reference Requirements

Where a document's principle is later operationalized by another document at a different level, the operationalizing document shall cite its source.

Cross-reference is not optional courtesy. It is how the repository keeps its own history honest about where its ideas came from.

---

# Living Principle

Repository architecture is itself a form of stewardship.

A reader five, fifty, or five hundred years from now should be able to open this repository and know, without guessing, which document governs a given question, why, and what came before it.

Every generation that adds to this repository is responsible for leaving that trust stronger, not weaker, than it was received.
